Privacy policy

Your photos stay on your device. The paperwork goes here. Because compliance and regulation are the new AI.

This policy covers Gleitzsch for iOS and Android, the browser photo editor and this project website, maintained by Bogdan Kirilenko.

Provider and postal contact details: Impressum.

Android is available as an early APK alpha. Some features described below are still iOS-only; the Android alpha records silent video and does not request microphone access.

Browser photo editor

The web editor processes a photograph, entered text and effect settings locally with WebGPU and WebAssembly. They are not uploaded. Saving downloads a separate PNG with the Gleitzsch name, version and project website in its metadata; the original file is unchanged. Record video encodes the processed preview and any added text locally into a silent H.264 MP4, without requesting camera, microphone or screen-sharing access. Recordings are limited to 10 minutes and 256 MiB of retained encoded data, with an earlier size stop for finalization. Encoded buffers are browser-managed local data; the latest completed clip remains available through a local download link until another recording or page departure. Closing before finalization discards the unfinished clip. Browsers may use temporary files, and downloads or chosen cloud syncing follow browser/OS rules. No recording is uploaded or stored in an app database. MP4 filenames include the Gleitzsch version and export time; the browser may add ordinary container metadata such as creation time, duration and codec information. Source EXIF, location and camera metadata are not copied. There is no account, analytics or automatic project saving. Closing or reloading discards the current edit. The browser may cache the program files, which do not contain photographs. The hosting provider handles ordinary requests to deliver those files, as described below.

Read the web editor privacy policy for processing, offline use, downloads and optional diagnostics. The app-specific permissions below apply to the native apps.

Image processing

Gleitzsch processes camera images, photographs you select and microphone audio on your device. The app does not upload your images, audio or effect settings to the developer or to a server. It does not require an account. We add no advertising, tracking, analytics or crash-reporting services of our own. Your operating system may handle diagnostics separately under your own system settings.

All image effects run on your device using conventional signal-processing algorithms. No AI, no neural networks. The app does not send images to an AI service or use them to train models.

Camera, microphone and photo library

Camera access provides the live preview and lets you create glitched photographs and videos. The app does not request location access.

On iOS, microphone access is requested when you first start a video. With permission, video includes the original microphone sound. Without permission, videos are silent. The current Android alpha does not request microphone access and records silent video.

Preview and photographs do not use the microphone. On iOS, microphone capture stops when video recording ends or you leave the camera. Audio is not saved separately from the video.

Gleitzsch receives the photograph you choose to open in the editor, processes a reduced copy in memory and leaves the original unchanged.

On iOS, you can choose a picture in Apple’s photo picker or share it through Edit in Gleitzsch. Apple may download the selected item from iCloud Photos. A temporary local copy of the selected file is used while opening it and removed after import.

On iOS, saving a result requests add-only access to Photos. This permits adding a new photograph or video; it does not grant general read access. On Android, saving adds a photograph or video to the device’s media library without granting access to the rest of your library.

Editor results are new PNG images made from the displayed pixels, without copying the source photo’s location or device metadata. Your photo library or gallery manages saved photographs and videos. It may sync them through your chosen cloud photo service if you have enabled that service; Gleitzsch does not perform that sync.

You can change permissions in your device’s system settings.

On iOS, saved photo and video file metadata includes the Gleitzsch app name, version and project website. On Android, camera photographs and PNG editor results include the same app name, version and project website metadata. Gleitzsch does not add author, location or user/device identifiers. Sharing services may remove this metadata.

Local settings and saved mixes

C1–C3 mixes are stored locally. The camera remembers which favorite was active. Reset clears the current effect settings. Saved slots remain until you replace or delete them. On iOS, these local settings may be included in device backups managed by the operating system. The current Android port disables backup and device transfer for its private app data.

Deleting a mix does not delete photographs or videos already saved to your photo library. Manage those files in your photo library or gallery.

iOS distribution

Gleitzsch is not currently distributed through Apple’s App Store or TestFlight. The developer does not currently receive TestFlight usage data, crash reports or beta feedback. This policy will be updated before such distribution begins.

Support, licensing and public issues

For support, privacy or commercial licensing questions, email gleitzsch@mail.online-impressum.de. If you email the developer, the developer receives your email address, message and any attachments through the email services used to deliver and receive your message and uses them to answer your request. Licensing correspondence may also include your project or organisation details, proposed use, information needed to establish whether a separate agreement is required, and agreed permissions or fees. If you choose to email a link or your creative work, the developer uses it to view what you made and respond to your message. Permission will be requested separately before featuring your work publicly. The support mailbox is supplied by Online-Impressum (Clear-Media UG (haftungsbeschränkt)) and its contracted hosting providers. The developer reads it through WebMail. Support messages are not automatically forwarded to an AI assistant. Postal letters sent to the Impressum contact address may also be received and digitized by Online-Impressum to pass your correspondence to the developer. Its data processing agreement describes the contracted processing. The in-app support draft includes the app and operating system versions; you can edit it before sending. Photographs and videos are not attached automatically. The retention criteria below apply to correspondence and any diagnostic files or media you choose to send. Please avoid including personal information unrelated to your request.

For privacy requests concerning data held by the developer, use the same email address. Your rights are described below.

Public bug reports and ideas can be posted through GitHub Issues. If you post an issue, your GitHub profile, message and attachments are public. Only share material you intend to publish. A GitHub account is required to post; it is not required to use the app.

Issue content is used to answer questions and investigate bugs and may remain as a public project record after an issue is closed. GitHub manages its storage and account data under its privacy statement. Use email for private support and privacy requests.

This website

This static website can be served through GitHub Pages or Cloudflare Pages. It contains no analytics scripts, advertising, embedded social feeds, third-party fonts, forms, cookies or browser storage set by this site.

When served through Cloudflare Pages, Cloudflare receives technical request information, such as your IP address, to deliver the pages and editor files. These requests do not contain your photographs, entered text or effect settings. Cloudflare’s privacy policy applies to that hosting. When served through GitHub Pages, GitHub receives technical request information to deliver the pages and files. APK downloads remain hosted on GitHub Pages, including when reached from the Cloudflare site. Downloading the APK does not register you for Google Play testing or create a Gleitzsch account. The developer does not collect APK download statistics. GitHub’s privacy statement applies to that hosting. The Impressum link opens an external page hosted by Online-Impressum; its privacy policy applies there. Following a link to GitHub, Telegram, Apple or another service takes you to a service with its own privacy practices.

Purposes and legal bases

The developer is the controller for personal data handled to operate this project and respond to you. Ordinary technical requests are processed to deliver and protect the website and downloads. Support correspondence, voluntarily shared diagnostics and issue reports are used to answer requests, investigate reported problems and maintain useful technical solutions. Creative work and links you voluntarily share by email are used to view what you made and respond to you. The legal basis for these activities is our legitimate interest under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR in providing a functioning, secure project and responding to people who contact us. We limit processing to what these purposes require.

Commercial licensing correspondence is used to discuss proposed uses, agree permissions and any fees, and administer resulting licences. Article 6(1)(b) GDPR applies where this is necessary to take steps at your request before entering into a contract with you or to perform that contract. For contacts acting on behalf of an organisation, Article 6(1)(f) GDPR applies to our legitimate interest in handling business correspondence and licensing. Where particular records must be kept to comply with a legal obligation, Article 6(1)(c) GDPR applies to those records. Using the app or editor does not require you to provide the developer with a photograph or create an account. Contacting support and sharing diagnostics are optional; without the relevant contact information or problem details, we may be unable to reply or investigate. We do not use personal data for profiling or decisions with legal or similarly significant effects.

Retention

Ordinary support and creative-work emails, routine licensing enquiries and their attachments are scheduled for automatic deletion when more than 30 weeks (210 days) old. This applies across all mailbox folders, including received and sent messages and trash. The period concerns the age of each message, rather than the date a support request is closed. We use this limited retention period to handle related follow-up questions and investigate reported problems. Necessary records of agreed permissions and licences are kept separately for the duration of the licence and any applicable statutory retention or legal claims period; only records needed for those purposes are retained.

Unneeded attachments may be removed earlier. Where an erasure request must be granted, we delete the relevant data without waiting for routine automatic deletion. If a specific legal obligation or an actual legal claim requires longer retention, only the necessary records are kept separately for that purpose. For a legal claim, retention ends when the matter and applicable claims periods have ended; mandatory records follow their applicable statutory retention period. Postal correspondence is kept while its purpose and any directly related unresolved follow-up continue, subject to these legal exceptions.

A public issue may remain as a useful technical project record after it is closed. Personal details that are no longer necessary can be removed; contact us about personal data in an issue. Copies made by other people and GitHub’s own platform records are governed separately. Hosting logs and provider backups follow the applicable provider’s retention and deletion cycles and may outlast deletion from an active mailbox. The developer does not set an independent retention period for providers’ own security or account records.

Recipients and international transfers

Recipients depend on how you interact with the project: Cloudflare delivers the main website and editor; GitHub delivers the mirror and APK downloads and operates public Issues; Online-Impressum and its hosting providers handle the support mailbox and postal contact service. These services do not receive your locally edited photographs from Gleitzsch. A file you deliberately attach to a support message or public report is shared with the recipients of that message or report.

Cloudflare and GitHub operate internationally, including in the United States. Technical request data and data you choose to submit to GitHub may therefore be processed outside the European Economic Area. Cloudflare’s DPA, section 6, describes its EU–US Data Privacy Framework and, where applicable, Standard Contractual Clauses. GitHub describes its transfer safeguards, including the Data Privacy Framework and Standard Contractual Clauses, in its privacy statement. These documents explain the safeguards; you can also contact us for information about those applicable to the project. Online-Impressum’s AVV describes its processing locations and subprocessors. Following an external link, including to Telegram, is governed by the destination’s own practices.

Your data protection rights

Subject to the applicable conditions, you can request access to, correction or erasure of your personal data, restriction of processing, and data portability. You can object to processing based on Article 6(1)(f) GDPR on grounds relating to your particular situation. Where processing relies on consent, you can withdraw it at any time without affecting the lawfulness of processing before withdrawal.

Send requests to gleitzsch@mail.online-impressum.de. We may need proportionate information to verify that the request concerns your data. We will respond within one month; if a lawful extension is needed, we will explain it within that month. You also have the right to complain to a supervisory authority, particularly where you live or work or where you believe an infringement occurred. The Berlin data protection authority provides information and a complaint channel.
